# Conversion and content

Conversion-quality UX reduces uncertainty rather than manipulating users. Explain what users receive, the real price, when a charge begins, cancellation terms, timing, and safeguards before asking for commitment.

Prefer action labels that describe outcomes: “Start free trial,” “Reserve for 14 June,” or “Pay ₹1,240.” Use a generic “Continue” only when the destination is already unmistakable.

Write with concrete, truthful benefits. Short headings orient; body copy removes doubts; labels clarify; error messages explain recovery. Avoid vague superlatives, fake social proof, unsupported comparisons, and long promotional paragraphs that hide operational details.

For content resilience, test short and long titles, absent ratings/images, large and zero values, alternate currencies and dates, translated text, and error copy. Do not truncate information that is necessary to decide or recover.

## Trust copy placement

Place an explanation next to the moment it matters rather than in a distant FAQ:

| User question | Helpful evidence or copy |
| --- | --- |
| What am I getting? | Specific scope, limits, and expected outcome. |
| What will it cost? | A real total and any known recurring or additional charge. |
| Can I change my mind? | Cancellation, return, edit, or refund conditions. |
| What happens after this? | A concise next-step preview and timing. |
| Is my data safe? | A truthful explanation of the relevant permission or privacy boundary. |

Use reassurance to clarify a real policy, never as decoration. Avoid urgency timers, preselected expensive options, hidden fees, confusing opt-outs, or wording that makes declining feel like an error.
