# Product-page UX

For product, booking, pricing, checkout, or subscription screens, help the visitor answer in order: what is this, is it trustworthy, what does it cost, what exactly happens next, and what can I change later?

Keep identity, rating/trust evidence, price, variant/quantity, and primary purchase action visually connected. Show confirmed totals where possible, place cancellation or renewal terms near commitment, and use honest specific copy instead of urgency tricks.

Use a consistent image system: stable aspect ratios, purposeful cropping, sensible fallbacks, and contrast-safe overlay controls. Do not let favorite/share/navigation controls disappear against bright or busy imagery.

Common fixes:

- Put ratings close to identity when they help evaluation.
- Replace unclear price ranges with the actual total where it is known.
- Keep quantity near purchase action and label it as purchase amount rather than product identity.
- Use quiet dividers and meaningful grouping instead of nested cards.
- Consider persistent context or an action area only on genuinely long, high-intent flows.

Never invent discounts, reviews, scarcity, delivery claims, or trust metrics.

## Purchase-flow checklist

Before shipping, confirm these are visible without a scavenger hunt:

- Product or offer identity, meaningful image, price/total, availability, selected option, and primary action.
- What is included, delivery or access timing, taxes/fees when known, and cancellation/renewal terms.
- Clear quantity/variant selection and a summary that reflects the selected values.
- Validation next to the relevant field, a recoverable payment/error state, and a confirmation state with next steps.
- An accessible mobile order: identity → evaluation/trust → selection → price → commitment → reassurance.

Use sticky actions only when they retain essential context on a long, high-intent page. They must not hide content, trap keyboard focus, or obscure browser/system controls on mobile.
