# Jobs to be done

Identify the functional, emotional, and social progress that causes a user to choose, keep, replace, or reject a product.

## Workflow

1. Define the decision or product surface this research must inform; do not run JTBD as an abstract persona exercise.
2. Recruit around a recent real behavior: purchase, switch, abandonment, workaround, renewal, or cancellation.
3. Interview the timeline from first thought through active search, choice, use, and outcome. Capture push, pull, habit, anxiety, alternatives, and constraints.
4. Separate the core job from consumption steps, related jobs, desired outcomes, and solution preferences.
5. Cluster by circumstances and progress sought, not demographics alone. Preserve contradictory cases.
6. Translate findings into opportunity statements, onboarding promises, activation criteria, and hypotheses that remain testable.

## Deliverable

Return:
- job statement with circumstance and desired progress
- forces-of-progress timeline
- competing alternatives and workarounds
- desired outcomes and evidence strength
- product and experiment implications

## Guardrails

- Do not infer jobs from internal workshops alone.
- Do not turn requested features into the job statement.
- Use interview evidence and label unvalidated synthesis as a hypothesis.
