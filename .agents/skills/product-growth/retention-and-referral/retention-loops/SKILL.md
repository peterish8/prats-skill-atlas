# Retention loops

Connect a recurring user need to a trigger, action, value outcome, and credible reason to return.

## Workflow

1. Choose the product-specific usage interval and recurring job before selecting day-based retention metrics.
2. Analyze cohort retention curves, return frequency, value events, churn moments, and differences between retained and lapsed users.
3. Distinguish predictive behaviors from causal drivers and validate proposed mechanisms.
4. Map loop trigger, user action, immediate value, stored or accumulating value, and next natural trigger.
5. Remove loops based only on anxiety, streak loss, notification pressure, or meaningless engagement.
6. Prototype or test the smallest intervention and monitor value quality, fatigue, opt-out, and support effects.
7. Measure durable return and outcome completion over the relevant lifecycle.

## Deliverable

Return:
- retention baseline by cohort and interval
- repeat-value loop map
- behavior-to-retention evidence table
- intervention experiments
- fatigue and quality guardrails

## Guardrails

- Do not maximize time spent when the product's job is efficient completion.
- Avoid coercive streaks, loss framing, and notification dependency.
- Correlation between an action and retention does not prove forcing the action will retain users.
