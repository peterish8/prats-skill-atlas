# UX and behavioral journey brief

Adapt the size to the task; complete the relevant fields with actual decisions.

## Product contract

- Mode and authorized surfaces.
- User goal and real successful outcome.
- Audience/vocabulary and existing brand/navigation.
- Current flow and observed friction, with source/screen evidence where present.
- Existing draft, persistence, privacy, confirmation and retry boundaries.

## Engagement loop

Write the exact invitation → small action → visible consequence → meaning → result → optional exploration chain. Explain why the motif belongs to this product. State which parts are implementation facts versus hypotheses about user response.

## Journey map

| Beat/state | User intent | Prompt and helper copy | Small action | Accepted state | Visual consequence | Next action | Skip/back/edit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Cover pending, failure, retry, success and interruption where applicable. Label future benefits and illustrative data.

## State contracts

- What auto-advances and why it is low-risk/reversible.
- What requires explicit Continue or confirmation.
- Progress meaning: answered versus saved/complete.
- Current data versus effects already played.
- Back/edit/reload/detour restoration.
- Duplicate activation, stale timers and late response ownership.
- Success source, failure recovery and retained work.
- Accessible static/reduced-motion equivalent and input alternatives.

## Completion and closure

- Useful result or artifact, and any optional personalization.
- Save/share/finish/replay behavior; what data leaves the app and when.
- Result remains accessible if a showcase is skipped or a share is cancelled.
- One concrete next step without inventing new product promises.

## Behavioral validation

Favor task success, comprehension, error recovery and voluntary exploration. Specify approved measurement only; do not add tracking implicitly.
